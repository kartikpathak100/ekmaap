    How EkMaap works
    From one photo to a verifiable grade. All processing runs on this device; nothing is uploaded.
    
  
    
    
    
      
      
        
    1. Learn the sheetThe colour of the grading sheet is read from the photo edges.
      2. 
        
    2. Find every onionPixels that differ from the sheet are grouped into objects; specks are removed and holes filled.
      3. 
        
    3. Set the scaleThe printed card's known side gives millimetres per pixel.
      4. 
        
    4. Separate touching onionsA distance map finds each bulb's centre and splits the blob at the narrow contact.
      5. 
        
    5. MeasureNeck, roots and sprouts are trimmed; the widest diameter is reported in mm.
      6. 
        
    6. Read the surfaceShare of dark (rot, mould), green (sprout) and pale (exposed flesh) area, and outline solidity (cuts, splits).
      7. 
        
    7. Decide the conditionThresholds at first, then a classifier trained on officer corrections. Uncertain onions go to the officer.
      8. 
        
    8. Apply the rule setSize and condition give Grade A, URS or Reject; lot percentages follow.
      9. 
        
    9. Issue the reportPhoto and record fingerprints (SHA-256), time and location, verifiable by anyone.
      10. 
      
    
    
    
    
    
      
    
        
    Photographing a sample
    
        
    One layer of onions on a plain light sheet, not stacked. Scale card flat on the same sheet and fully visible. Phone held straight above. Daylight or diffuse light, no flash. For white onions, use a darker sheet.
    
      
    
      
    
        
    Scope
    
        
    Percentages are by count of onions photographed. A photo shows one side of each bulb; internal rot is confirmed by the officer's cut test, as today. Grade limits come from the active rule set.
