What happens to a photo, step by step

    
    
      
  1. Find the sheet colour. The app looks at the edges of the photo and learns the colour of the sheet.
    2. 
      
  2. Find the onions. Every pixel clearly different in colour from the sheet (or much darker) is marked as "object". Small specks are removed and holes are filled.
    3. 
      
  3. Find the size card. The black square is recognised by its shape (square, solid, dark). Its known side length in mm gives the scale: how many pixels make one millimetre.
    4. 
      
  4. Separate touching onions. If a blob is not a clean convex shape, the app measures how far each pixel is from the blob's edge. The "centres" of two touching onions are far from the edge, the contact point is not, so the blob splits there.
    5. 
      
  5. Measure. Thin parts (neck, roots, sprout) are trimmed off, then the widest distance across the onion is converted to mm.
    6. 
      
  6. Describe the surface. For each onion the app computes the share of very dark pixels (rot, black mould), green pixels (sprout), pale pixels (peeled or cut flesh) and how convex the outline is (splits, cuts, misshapes).
    7. 
      
  7. Decide the condition. At first, fixed starting rules decide sound / sprouted / damaged / rotten. Once you have labelled enough onions, a k-nearest-neighbour classifier trained on your labels takes over. Onions near a threshold, or with a split vote, go to Needs review instead of being graded silently.
    8. 
      
  8. Apply the rule set. Condition and size are turned into Grade A, URS or Reject using the editable rule set, and the lot percentages are counted.
    9. 
      
  9. Report. The report records the photo fingerprint (SHA-256), every onion's result, the rule-set version, time and optional GPS, plus a fingerprint of the whole record. Anyone can re-check it on the Verify card.
    10. 
    

  

  

    
Known limits of this prototype

    
    
      
  • Not yet tested on real onion photos. The starting heuristics are fixed values, not learned from data.
    • 
      
  • Grade limits (45 mm, 35 mm) are demo values from 2026 news reports. The official specification must be confirmed.
    • 
      
  • Percentages are by count, not by weight.
    • 
      
  • A photo shows one side of each onion; defects on the hidden side and internal rot are not visible.
    • 
      
  • Needs a plain light sheet. White onions on a white sheet will not be separated; use a darker sheet for them (and re-check the sensitivity sliders).
    • 
      
  • The size assumes the onions and the card lie on the same flat sheet and the phone is held straight above.
    • 
      
  • Everything runs inside this page on the phone; nothing is uploaded. Labels and reports are lost when the page closes unless downloaded.
