# Field research plan — officer and farmer interviews

Purpose: learn how onion grading is actually done at a procurement centre, so the grading rules, the mat and the interface can be checked against real practice. Nothing in this document is a finding; it is the plan and the question list.

## Who to ask
- A grading or procurement officer at a procurement centre or mandi (2–3 people if possible)
- A farmer who sells onions through such a centre (2–3 people)
- A trader or commission agent, if available

## Before the visit
- Ask permission to talk, and to photograph onions on a mat. Do not photograph people without consent.
- Carry the printed A4 mat (`python scripts/make_mat.py`) and the 50 mm reference card.
- Note date, place and the role of each person (not their name unless they agree).

## Questions for a grading officer
1. How do you grade a lot today, step by step? How many onions do you look at?
2. What do you check: size, colour, shape, sprouting, rot, dryness, neck, skin?
3. Where do the size limits for Grade A and URS come from? Is there a written specification you use?
4. How long does grading one lot take?
5. How often do you and a colleague disagree on a lot? What happens then?
6. How is a farmer's complaint handled today, and what record exists?
7. What phone do you use, and is there mobile network at the centre?
8. In which language would you want to read the result? Who would use the app, you or an assistant?
9. If an app showed an onion it was unsure about and asked you to decide, would that be acceptable? How many such questions would be too many?
10. What would make you stop using such an app?

## Questions for a farmer
1. How is your onion graded when you sell? Do you see how it is done?
2. Have you ever disagreed with a grade? What happened?
3. Would a printed report with a QR code help you? Who would you show it to?
4. Which language do you read most easily? Can you read on a phone screen?
5. What would you want to be shown about your lot after grading?
